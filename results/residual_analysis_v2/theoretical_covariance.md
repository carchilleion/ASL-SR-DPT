## 1. Derivation of Measurement Noise Covariance

In ASL-SR-DPT Variant A6, compressive sampling is applied exclusively to the 63 AC Discrete Cosine Transform coefficients:
$$y_{\text{ac}} = A_{\text{ac}} \theta_{\text{ac, clean}} + e_{\text{ac}}$$
where $A_{\text{ac}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M_{\text{ac}} \times N_{\text{ac}}}$ with $M_{\text{ac}} = 37$ and $N_{\text{ac}} = 63$.

The projected measurement noise vector is:
$$e_{\text{ac}} = A_{\text{ac}} n_{\text{ac}}$$
where $n_{\text{ac}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{norm}}^2 I_{N_{\text{ac}}})$ is the white Gaussian noise in the orthonormal DCT domain.

Because linear transformations of Gaussian vectors remain Gaussian:
$$\mathbb{E}[e_{\text{ac}}] = A_{\text{ac}} \mathbb{E}[n_{\text{ac}}] = 0$$
$$\Sigma_A = \operatorname{Cov}(e_{\text{ac}}) = \mathbb{E}[e_{\text{ac}} e_{\text{ac}}^T] = A_{\text{ac}} \mathbb{E}[n_{\text{ac}} n_{\text{ac}}^T] A_{\text{ac}}^T = \sigma_{\text{norm}}^2 A_{\text{ac}} A_{\text{ac}}^T$$

Let $G = A_{\text{ac}} A_{\text{ac}}^T \in \mathbb{R}^{37 \times 37}$ denote the sensing Gram matrix. Then:
$$\Sigma_A = \sigma_{\text{norm}}^2 G$$

## 2. Row Normalization vs. Orthogonality

In `sensing.py`, the sensing matrix is generated by drawing standard normal entries and normalizing each row to have unit Euclidean norm:
$$A_{i,:} \leftarrow \frac{A_{i,:}}{\|A_{i,:}\|_2}$$

### 2.1 The Diagonal Elements of $G$
For any row $i$:
$$G_{i,i} = \langle A_{i,:}, A_{i,:} \rangle = \|A_{i,:}\|_2^2 = 1.0 \quad (\text{strictly, to machine precision})$$
Therefore, the marginal variance of every individual compressive measurement is strictly preserved:
$$\operatorname{Var}((e_{\text{ac}})_i) = (\Sigma_A)_{i,i} = \sigma_{\text{norm}}^2 G_{i,i} = \sigma_{\text{norm}}^2$$
**Row normalization guarantees that no single measurement carries amplified or attenuated noise.**

### 2.2 The Off-Diagonal Elements of $G$
For distinct rows $i \neq j$, $A_{i,:}$ and $A_{j,:}$ are independent random unit vectors uniformly distributed on the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^{62} \subset \mathbb{R}^{63}$.  
From geometric probability, their inner product has expectation and variance:
$$\mathbb{E}[G_{i,j}] = 0$$
$$\operatorname{Var}(G_{i,j}) = \frac{1}{N_{\text{ac}}} = \frac{1}{63} \approx 0.015873$$
The expected absolute correlation between any two distinct measurement rows is:
$$\mathbb{E}[|G_{i,j}|] = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi N_{\text{ac}}}} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{63 \pi}} \approx 0.10055$$

## 3. The True Distribution of the Residual Norm $\|e_{\text{ac}}\|_2$

Because $G$ is symmetric positive definite, it possesses an orthogonal eigendecomposition:
$$G = V \Lambda V^T, \quad \Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_{37})$$
where $\lambda_i > 0$ and $\sum_{i=1}^{37} \lambda_i = \operatorname{Tr}(G) = 37$.

Let $u = V^T e_{\text{ac}}$. Then $u \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{norm}}^2 \Lambda)$, meaning coordinates $u_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{norm}}^2 \lambda_i)$ are mutually independent.  
The squared Euclidean norm is invariant under orthogonal rotation:
$$\|e_{\text{ac}}\|_2^2 = e_{\text{ac}}^T e_{\text{ac}} = u^T u = \sum_{i=1}^{37} u_i^2 = \sigma_{\text{norm}}^2 \sum_{i=1}^{37} \lambda_i Z_i^2$$
where $Z_i \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$.

### 3.1 Generalized Chi-Square Formulation
The variable:
$$Q = \frac{\|e_{\text{ac}}\|_2^2}{\sigma_{\text{norm}}^2} = \sum_{i=1}^{37} \lambda_i Z_i^2$$
follows a **Generalized Chi-Square Distribution** (specifically, a non-centrality zero positive linear combination of independent $\chi^2(1)$ variables).

### 3.2 Exact Moments:
1. **Expected Value:**
   $$\mathbb{E}[Q] = \sum_{i=1}^{37} \lambda_i \mathbb{E}[Z_i^2] = \sum_{i=1}^{37} \lambda_i = \operatorname{Tr}(G) = 37$$
   $$\mathbb{E}[\|e_{\text{ac}}\|_2^2] = 37 \sigma_{\text{norm}}^2$$
   **CRITICAL INSIGHT:** The expected squared residual is **EXACTLY EQUAL** to the ordinary chi-square expectation $M_{\text{ac}} \sigma_{\text{norm}}^2$!  
   The non-orthogonality of $A_{\text{ac}}$ does not alter the mean squared energy of the noise floor.

2. **Variance of the Squared Norm:**
   $$\operatorname{Var}(Q) = \sum_{i=1}^{37} \lambda_i^2 \operatorname{Var}(Z_i^2) = 2 \sum_{i=1}^{37} \lambda_i^2 = 2 \operatorname{Tr}(G^2) = 2 \|G\|_F^2$$
   Notice that $\|G\|_F^2 = \operatorname{Tr}(I) + \|G - I\|_F^2 = 37 + (4.595)^2 \approx 37 + 21.11 = 58.11$.  
   Therefore:
   $$\operatorname{Var}(Q) \approx 2 \times 58.11 = 116.22$$
   In contrast, for an ordinary $\chi^2(37)$ variable with independent coordinates ($G = I$), the variance is $2 \times 37 = 74.0$.  
   **The correlated sensing matrix increases the variance of the squared noise norm by $57\%$.**

3. **Standard Deviation of the Euclidean Norm $\|e_{\text{ac}}\|_2$:**
   $$\operatorname{SD}(\|e_{\text{ac}}\|_2) \approx \frac{\operatorname{SD}(Q)}{2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[Q]}} \sigma_{\text{norm}} = \frac{\sqrt{116.22}}{2\sqrt{37}} \sigma_{\text{norm}} \approx 0.886 \sigma_{\text{norm}}$$
   compared to $0.707 \sigma_{\text{norm}}$ for ordinary Chi.  
   The standard deviation of $\|e_{\text{ac}}\|_2$ is approximately **$1.23\times$ wider** than the ordinary Chi approximation.
