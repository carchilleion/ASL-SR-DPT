# Chapter 2: Research Methodology & Experimental Design

Chapter 2 Research Design This study employs a quantitative, experimental research design utilizing algorithmic simulation (in-silico sandbox experiments) to evaluate the performance of the ASL-SR-DPT. According to the Father Saturnino Urios University (FSUU) Institutional Student Research Manual, an experimental simulation design is highly appropriate for technological investigations where mathematical structures and algorithmic variables are systematically manipulated to observe their causal effects on system efficiency and output quality. The mathematical and logical structure of the sparse recovery pipeline represents the independent variables, which are manipulated across three primary configurations: Single-Loop continuation schedule (adapted from the ISSL0 framework) versus classical double-loop architectures; Dynamic Parameter Tuning (DPT), implemented through analytical gradient evaluation, Armijo-type backtracking, and controlled continuation of the smoothing parameter sigma, is evaluated against fixed-step and static-parameter approaches. Active-Set Support Restriction (Zero-Element Neglect, adapted from FAL0 with periodic support re-evaluation) versus dense matrix-vector updates. The dependent variables are divided into two primary evaluation domains: Computational Efficiency: Measured quantitatively by iterative solve latency (seconds) and the average cardinality of active support sets (indicative of Floating-Point Operation, or FLOP, reductions). Reconstruction Fidelity: Measured quantitatively by spatial Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR, in decibels), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and spatial Mean Squared Error (MSE) evaluated on the fully aggregated spatial images. To ensure academic and scientific rigor, the proposed algorithm is benchmarked directly against two established, peer-reviewed sparse recovery baselines under identical operational constraints: Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP): A classic greedy algorithm utilizing column-normalized projections (Tropp & Gilbert, 2007). LASSO-ADMM: A convex relaxation-based Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers solver using stable Cholesky factorization steps and primal-dual residual convergence tracking (Boyd et al., 2011). By strictly manipulating the algorithms under identical noise levels and compressive measurement bounds, the research design provides a controlled basis for evaluating the reconstruction-quality and computational-cost trade-off among the proposed and baseline methods.

Sampling Technique Because this study evaluates a publicly available image benchmark rather than human participants, conventional participant-sampling procedures are not applicable. The complete BSD68 dataset is used for final evaluation. A separate validation subset of BSD68 images is used for preliminary testing, parameter selection, and ablation experiments. The final benchmark does not exclude images based on texture complexity, spatial entropy, or edge content. This ensures that the reported results represent the performance of ASL-SR-DPT across the complete benchmark dataset rather than a selected subset of difficult or highly structured images. For the validation and preliminary experiments, the following criteria are applied: Format and Color Space: Images are processed as single-channel grayscale images. When source images are provided in another format, the luminance channel is extracted and converted to grayscale before processing. Image and Patch Structure: Images are divided into overlapping 8 × 8 pixel patches. Preliminary controlled experiments may include patches containing edges, contours, textures, gradients, and relatively uniform regions to evaluate solver behavior across different local image structures. Representativeness: The final evaluation includes all BSD68 images without purposive exclusion. This provides a broader assessment of reconstruction quality across natural image textures, structural boundaries, gradients, and varying local intensity distributions.
